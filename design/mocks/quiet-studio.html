Learning how to learnRevision 2

Remember more.
Read less.

Effective learning is less about consuming more information and more about working with what you already have. When you engage actively with a text, you give your brain more opportunities to notice what matters and to build lasting connections.

Active recall asks you to retrieve ideas from memory, rather than simply seeing them again.

Spaced practice builds on this by revisiting key ideas over time. The gaps between sessions are part of the learning, giving you a chance to discover what you can recall without help.

The most useful notes begin with a small piece of meaningful evidence. Capture the passage that changes how you think, add your own interpretation, and return to it when you need it.
